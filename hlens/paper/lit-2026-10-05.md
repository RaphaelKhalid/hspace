## 1. Second-order interpretability and the closest prior art to "SwiGLU neurons as bilinear atoms decoded via a lens"

| Title | Authors | Date | Link | Relevance | Key number |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bilinear MLPs enable weight-based mechanistic interpretability | Pearce, Dooms, Rigg, Oramas, Sharkey | 2024-10-10 (ICLR 2025) | [2410.08417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08417) | **Closest prior art.** For a bilinear GLU (no SiLU), the interaction matrix for an output direction v is Σ_i (v·w_down,i)·sym(w_gate,i w_up,iᵀ). That equals the Hessian of v·MLP(x), so each neuron contributes a rank-≤2 atom. They eigendecompose it per output/feature direction. Our SwiGLU atom is the SiLU-weighted, input-dependent generalization. Setting SiLU′=1 and SiLU″=0 gives back their object exactly. | TinyLlama-1.1B can be fine-tuned to bilinear at "modest loss" [abstract/search] |
| Converting MLPs into Polynomials in Closed Form | Belrose, Rigg | 2025-02-03 | [2502.01032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01032) | **Second-closest, and a likely reviewer objection.** It gives closed-form least-squares quadratic approximations of MLPs/GLUs and eigendecomposes the quadratic term. Under Gaussian inputs, the least-squares quadratic coefficient is E[Hessian]/2 (Stein's lemma), so this is effectively a data-averaged single-block Hessian. It has no lens/vocabulary decoding and no downstream transport. [abstract only] | Quadratic fits explain >95% of output variance |
| Finding Manifolds With Bilinear Autoencoders | Dooms, Gauderis | 2025-10-19 | [2510.16820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16820) | Activations are decomposed into quadratic (bilinear) latents that can be analysed input-independently. It is the dictionary-learning cousin of our atoms. [abstract only] | none in abstract |
| Bilinear autoencoders find interpretable manifolds | Dooms, Gauderis, Wiggins, Oramas | 2026-05-09 | [2605.08891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08891) | The same idea, scaled up, with an online visualizer for **Qwen 3.5**. It covers quadratic latents in residual activations. It does not do output-Hessian lenses. [abstract only] | Reports systematically lower reconstruction error than linear SAEs |
| R-lens: Making J-lens more faithful on early layers | camilablank, agam_bhatia, Neel Nanda | 2026-08-05 | [AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/nv8oedrnLXKRzNEL9/r-lens-making-j-lens-more-faithful-on-early-layers) | It replaces the J-lens backward pass with LRP rules: LayerNorm treated as constant, SiLU/GELU detached, and a **half-rule that splits relevance evenly across multiplicative gate branches**. That is a first-order rule for the same SwiGLU gate product our atoms describe. Tested on Qwen3.6-27B and DeepSeek-V4-Flash. [post-summ] | Pass@10 advantage over J-lens grows with scale |
| Backward Lens: Projecting LM gradients into the vocabulary space | Katz, Belinkov, Geva, Wolf | 2024-02 (EMNLP 2024) | [2402.12865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12865); [TransformerLens issue on gate/up/down factors](https://github.com/TransformerLensOrg/TransformerLens/issues/1832) | It decodes gradient factors (including the gated-MLP gate, up and down factors) through the unembedding. This is a precedent for "decode derivative factors via vocabulary projection". It is first-order and about weight gradients. [not re-read this sweep] | n/a |
| Hessian-Enhanced Token Attribution (HETA) | Pramanik, Maliha, Bastian, Jha | 2026-04-14 (ICLR 2026) | [2604.13258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13258) | New detail: it runs HVPs (Hutchinson, rank-64 blockwise) on **Llama-3.1-70B**, but only in the last six layers. The Hessian is taken w.r.t. input embeddings, not the residual-stream output direction. [html-summ] | DSA 5.10 vs 3.78 for ReAgent (Llama) |
| When Attribution Patching Lies: diagnosis and a second-order correction | Zhang, Wang | 2026-06-05 | [2606.09899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09899) | A per-prompt HVP correction to attribution patching. It is not averaged and not decoded through a lens. Details are in Q5. [full] | Up to 82% error reduction (Llama-3.1-8B IOI, multi-step HVP) |
| Beyond Linear Probes: Truncated Polynomial Classifiers (TPC) | Oldfield, Torr, Patras, Bibi et al. | 2025-09-30 (ICLR 2026) | [2509.26238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26238) | Polynomial (2nd-and-higher-order) activation probes for safety monitoring. This is the trained-monitor counterpart of a quadratic H-lens score and is a baseline if we pitch "quadratic monitor". [abstract only] | WildGuardMix, 4 models up to 30B; matches or beats MLP probes |
| OmniLens: Interpreting LM hidden states at scale | Pettyjohn, Sakarvadia, Hudson et al. | 2026-08-10 | [2608.10260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10260) | A tuned-lens ensemble (482 lenses on Llama-3.3-70B). It is first-order and learned, and is relevant only as a "lens at 70B" scale precedent. [abstract only] | 98.4% fewer parameters via low-rank translators |
| Short Horizons and Sparse Concepts: a mathematical view of the J-lens readout | Yan, Ding, Tan et al. | 2026-08-26 | [2608.25347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25347) | Analyses J-lens as the optimal first-order local linear transfer and discusses its global approximation bias. It does not go to second order, so our curvature term is its natural next step. [abstract only] | n/a |

**Is anything identical?** Not found. I found no paper or post that:
- takes the corpus-averaged Hessian of an output direction w.r.t. the layer-l residual in a standard (SiLU) SwiGLU transformer,
- writes it as a sum of per-neuron rank-2 gate/up atoms, and
- decodes those atoms through the J-lens/unembedding.

Pearce et al. (bilinear, weight-only, no averaging and no transport) is the exact special case. Belrose & Rigg (averaged quadratic of one block, no lens) is the data-averaged single-block analogue. R-lens's half-rule is the first-order treatment of the same gate.

No new 2025–2026 Shapley-Taylor or integrated-Hessian work on LLM residual streams turned up beyond the Oct 4 sweep.

**Current best multi-token white-box reader:** Concept Lens with R-lens transport, which is training-free apart from a 0.1 A100-h calibration.
- Average Rank@10 is 54.5% across five models, and 40.3% on Qwen3.6-27B end-to-end.
- The bottleneck is proposal coverage, not scoring: 90.6% when the target is supplied.
- It reads 1–4-token concepts. I found **no published training-free method that reads sentence-length (~25-token) content**, such as plan descriptions, out of J-space. Free-text readers (Oracle Lens, NLA, AVPO) are trained, and at a matched layer the Oracle Lens is no better than Concept Lens.

**SOTA summary:**
- **Black-box agentic monitoring:** frontier monitors reach AUC ≈0.87 on SHADE-Arena and catch 60–95% on BashArena-derived MonitoringBench, depending on attack refinement. The open-weight SOTA is the trained Qwen3.5-27B deliberative monitor (pAUROC@20 0.831, OOD).
- **Protocols:** resample-plus-defer reaches 96% safety on SHADE-Arena; Ctrl-Z brings attack success to 7%.
- **White-box:** strong in narrower, mostly single-turn or in-distribution settings (deception probes 95–99% @1% FPR; HackTrace 0.997; ObserverBench 0.979 on APPS).
- **Gap:** I found **no 2026 paper that reports safety at a fixed audit budget for probes or lenses on the untrusted agent's own activations in BashArena, LinuxArena or SHADE-Arena.** The closest are Hua et al. (code review, 2025) and ObserverBench (APPS, single-step). Not found beyond these.

## What this means for us

1. **Novelty risk (high):** the per-neuron rank-2 atom is *exactly* Pearce et al.'s bilinear interaction matrix when SiLU→identity. Claim only:
   - the SwiGLU generalization (SiLU′/SiLU″ input-dependent coefficients),
   - corpus averaging,
   - transport and decoding through J-lens/R-lens,
   - use as a monitor.

   Cite Pearce 2410.08417, Belrose & Rigg 2502.01032 and bilinear AEs 2605.08891 up front.
2. Pre-empt the "averaged Hessian = least-squares quadratic (Stein)" objection. Belrose & Rigg already do the single-block averaged quadratic, so our difference is downstream transport plus the vocabulary readout.
3. R-lens beats J-lens everywhere (Concept Lens on Qwen3.6-27B: 40.3% vs 23.1%), and R-lens's half-rule *is* a gate rule. Report an R-lens-transported H-lens and an R-lens first-order baseline, not just J-lens.
4. The multi-token baseline to beat is Concept Lens (R): 40.3% Rank@10 on Qwen3.6-27B end-to-end, 90.6% scoring-only. Its concepts are 1–4 tokens. There is **no published training-free 25-token or sentence reader**, so the `longdesc` question is still open.
5. Trained free-text readers are not clearly better: Oracle Lens gets 2.17% vs R-lens 2.79% at matched L44. NLAs have plausibility and confabulation issues (21%→7.6%; Flow-NLA, AVPO). That weakens "just use an NLA" as a reviewer counter.
6. The monitor baselines we must beat at a **fixed 1% audit budget**:
   - a trained linear probe on the same Qwen activations,
   - the trained Qwen3.5-27B black-box monitor (pAUROC@20 0.831),
   - a Neyman-Pearson combination of both (Hua et al.).

   Report the add-on gain over the black-box monitor, not raw AUROC (ObserverBench shows AUROC can mis-rank monitors).
7. Our Oct 5 negative (zero-shot J-lens add-on AUROC 0.48) is consistent with Parrack et al.'s "weak black-to-white boosts". Frame it as a calibrated negative result and include the cross-lineage (41% vs 19%) and attack-selection (−20 to −28 pp) caveats.
8. Data: there are **no public Qwen-27B agentic sabotage trajectories.** The cheapest paths are:
   - (a) the 750/750 `control_arena_{bash,shade,iac,agentdojo}` sets as off-policy text for a Qwen3.6-27B surrogate monitor;
   - (b) on-policy generation with `qwen/qwen3.6-27b` (about $0.30/M input, $2–3.25/M output) or `qwen3.8-27b` (DeepInfra bf16 at $0.15/$1.875) on BashArena, within the OpenRouter cap;
   - (c) sanity sets: NARCBench (Qwen3-32B activations, 1.46 GB) and Liars' Bench Qwen3.5-27B (gated, 16k rows).
9. Engineering:
   - Use activation-space HVPs (cheap: one double backward, about 3× a gradient) with fp32 gradients and SDPA on the math backend (or FlashBoB).
   - On 8 GB locally, restrict to a layer window, as HETA did on 70B (last 6 layers).
   - Do not use bf16 finite differences (error about 1e-2).
10. To stay defensible, our claim should be: "the first corpus-averaged second-order (H-)lens for SwiGLU transformers, decomposed into gate×up atoms readable through J/R-space and evaluated as a fixed-budget add-on monitor". Avoid "first second-order interpretability" (HETA, HVP-AP, TPC, bilinear MLPs) and "first lens monitor" (J-lens paper, workspace-bench).
