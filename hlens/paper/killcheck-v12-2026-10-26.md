## The idea, restated

Fix residual directions u_i at a mid layer. For a pair, compute the 2x2 factorial difference
r_ij(x) = h_L(x+u_i+u_j) - h_L(x+u_i) - h_L(x+u_j) + h_L(x)
at the nudged token. Average over unrelated contexts. Test split-half context invariance, test that it is not a single-move effect, decode through W_U, and compare H pairs against covariance-matched decoy pairs and J-space pairs.

## Verdict: OPEN (method is textbook, measurement is unclaimed)

The estimator is not new. It is the discrete mixed second difference. With a fixed baseline it is the 2-player Shapley-Taylor interaction, and it is the finite-step version of a Hessian contraction. What I could not find anywhere is this estimator applied to **internal residual directions**, **averaged over contexts into a fixed vector**, and **decoded through the unembedding**. Nobody reports a context-invariant "joint meaning" of a direction pair.

Deciding works:
1. **Gurnee, Sofroniew et al. 2026 (J-lens).** First order only. They use two J-lens vectors at once in swaps and k-vector ablations and injections, but never measure an interaction term. They list structure "beyond a flat collection" as future work. This is the scoop risk, not a scoop.
2. **Todd et al. 2024 (function vectors).** Summed function vectors trigger composite tasks "to some extent". That is the opposite prior: composition is roughly additive. Kang et al. 2026 add that attribute steering vectors compose additively and are near-orthogonal. Expect small emergent terms.
3. **Singhvi et al. 2025 (Shapley-Taylor interactions in LMs).** Same estimator family, applied to input tokens and scalar outputs. No internal directions, no vocab decoding.

Surviving differentiator: a context-averaged, vector-valued pair interaction of mid-layer directions, decoded to tokens, with a test that its content is **pair-specific** (not a generic template shared by all pairs). The last part is the only thing that would make it a finding. See the facial flaw below.

## The most likely reason the idea is wrong on its face

**The average r_ij is dominated by a pair-generic template, so every pair, including twins, will look context-invariant and "emergent".**

Mechanism 1: norm curvature. Each block reads N(z) = sqrt(d) z / |z|. Its second derivative is
D²N[a, b] = -(sqrt(d)/ρ²) [ (a·b) n + (n·a) b + (n·b) a - 3 (n·a)(n·b) n ],
with ρ = |z| and n = z/ρ. I checked this formula numerically (max error 5e-4 against finite differences at step 1e-3). Here a and b are the two moves pushed forward to that norm's input. Two consequences:
- The (a·b) n and 3(n·a)(n·b) n terms point along the current state. Averaged over contexts, n becomes the mean residual direction. In large LLMs that is dominated by near-constant massive-activation dims (Sun et al. 2024, "Massive Activations", TODO-verify). So this part of r_ij is the same vector for every pair, scaled by a scalar. Split-half cosine will be high. It is not in the span of the single-move effects, so a naive "differs from single moves" test passes. Decoded through W_U it gives one fixed token list, likely frequent tokens or the model's default continuation, for every pair.
- It enters h_L through every downstream pre-norm (about 64 in Qwen3.6-27B), each time as the block's response to rescaling its own input. Averaged, that is a fixed vector per layer. The final norm adds the same term again at decode time.
- u_i and u_j are orthogonal at the injection layer, since they are eigenvectors of a symmetric operator. So the term vanishes at the first norm. It does not vanish downstream, because the pushforwards J u_i and J u_j are not orthogonal.
- Covariance-matched twins have similar pushforward overlaps. So they produce the same template and pass too. The H-vs-twin gap would then be magnitude only.

Mechanism 2: finite-step saturation. At non-tiny |u|, joint moves sub-add, giving r_ij ≈ -c (Δ_i + Δ_j). That has cosine about 0.7 with each single-move effect. A per-move cosine threshold can let it through. Only a projection test removes it.

Mechanism 3: selection. The u_i are the top-curvature directions by construction. They will beat covariance-matched decoys on interaction magnitude whatever the content. The project already measured 2.5 to 6x more interaction than twins (v8/v9). Magnitude is not evidence of joint meaning. Decoys must be curvature-matched, not covariance-matched.

Mechanism 4: decoding. Decoding r_ij through W_U norm(r_ij) rescales to unit length and discards sign and magnitude. Decode with the context-averaged linearized final norm instead, and include the final norm's own D²N term explicitly, or the decode is mostly the template.

## The single control that catches it

**Pair-identification on the de-templated residual.**
1. Build the pair-generic template m as the top principal direction of the averaged r over all pairs (H, J and decoy pairs pooled). Also take the averaged single-move effects Δ_i and Δ_j.
2. Form r̃_ij = r_ij projected off span{m, Δ_i, Δ_j}.
3. Split documents into disjoint halves A and B. For each pair, check whether r̃_ij from A is nearer to r̃_ij from B than to r̃_kl from B for every other pair kl. Report rank-1 identification accuracy against chance (1 / number of pairs), for H pairs, J pairs and curvature-matched decoys.

If the norm template drives the signal, raw split-half cosine stays high but identification falls to chance and r̃ has no reliable content. Only pair-specific, context-invariant content survives. That is exactly the claim "joint meaning neither carries alone".

Mechanism check, if identification fails: rerun with every RMSNorm denominator frozen at its clean-run value. That sets D²N to zero exactly. If r_ij mostly vanishes, the signal was norm curvature.

## What to do before building
1. Run the pair-identification control in the same script as the main result, not later.
2. Swap covariance-matched decoys for curvature-matched ones (matched on single-direction second-difference energy).
3. Use the projection test for "emergent", never per-move cosine.
4. Sweep step size and report r_ij / |u|². A stable ratio means a true second-order term. A growing one means saturation.
5. Cite Todd 2024, Kang 2026, Postmus and Abreu 2024 as the additive-composition prior. Cite Singhvi 2025 and STII (after verifying) for the estimator. Cite Marks 2024 and Engels 2025 for "pairs carry joint content". Cite the J-lens future-work line.
