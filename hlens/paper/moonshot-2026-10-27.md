# Is H-space real like J-space? Oct 26-28, 2026

Status: §1-§4k are exploratory and not preregistered. §4l-§4n are preregistered; each cites its PREREG file, and the freeze hashes are in `results/runlog_laptop.md`. Every number below can be recomputed from the scripts and JSON files cited.

Compute: laptop only, except §4j (A40 pod, about $0.67) and §4o (27B pod, about $1.43, no numbers).

## 1. The question, stated sharply
J-space is convincing because of the **mean**. The J-lens averages the Jacobian over contexts, and that average decodes to the same readable content in any context. The project's H-lens was *defined* the same way, one derivative up (theory-v2 §0): T[c] = E_x mean_p H_x[c]_pp.
- But every estimator it ran converges to a **second moment** over contexts: M^row, M^diag and the spatial-sign shapes (Prop. 6).
- Run 1's "M_lens" carried a Cov_x/B bias with B = 4 (Prop. 6f).
- So H-space as tested was "where curvature is large in *some* context". J-space is "where the effect is the same in *every* context".
- The context-invariant (J-like) part of the curvature had never been measured without that bias.

## 2. Result 1: the context-averaged Hessian at 27B is indistinguishable from zero
`hlens/hs_mlens_offline.py`, `results/mlens/mlens_offline.json`, `results/mlens_nodrop/mlens_offline.json`.

**Estimator.** Within one probe the covector c and direction z are shared. Rows from different windows are independent contexts.
- The product of window means across distinct windows is unbiased for M^lens = E_c T̃[c]². It carries no variance term.
- φ = tr M^lens / tr M^diag is the share of whitened per-position curvature energy that is context-invariant.

- The M^lens spectrum is symmetric: λ1 ≈ −λ_min at every layer.
- Nothing beats the exact per-probe PCA sign-flip null: off-diagonal share, λ1 of R, split-half.
- Top-5 eigenspaces do not replicate across runs (run 2 vs v8: 0.02 / 0.02 with the axis drop, 0.15 / 0.20 without).
- **Reading:** less than about 1% of per-position curvature energy at L16-L40 is the same across contexts. The mean H-lens that would be the true analog of the J-lens is empty at this resolution.

### 4b. The 0.8B mean H-lens, built and decoded
`hlens/hs_mlens08.py`, `results/mlens/mlens08_L18.json`, `out/mlens08_L18.log`. L18, 256 probes × 4 windows, 200 flips, 48 decode windows, 73 min CPU.

- **φ = 0.111.** This replicates §4's 0.106 with 16× more probes.
- **Spectrum.** The spectrum is one-sided: top eigenvalues 0.0095, 0.0078, 0.0054; bottom −0.00007. That is the opposite of 27B, where the spectrum is symmetric ±.
- **Stability.** Split-half top-3 is 0.52 (flip p = 0.015) and top-5 is 0.62 (p = 0.005). Top-1 fails (0.00) because λ1 ≈ λ2 lets the leading pair rotate.
- **Location.** The stable subspace sits mostly in the high-variance directions. Overlap with PCA5 is 0.56 and with PCA25 is 0.83.
- **Decoded content is generic.** Pair cross-terms r_ij = h(x+u_i+u_j) − h(x+u_i) − h(x+u_j) + h(x) have a held-in invariant fraction F_pairs = 0.109 for the mean-H directions. For 4 Σ-orbit twins F_pairs is 0.092-0.117. For singles F is 0.47 for both.
- Decodes look semi-coherent for both arms. Hmean pair 01 gives first names, pair 12 present-tense verbs, pair 14 past-tense verbs. tw0 pair 13 also gives first names, and tw0 bottoms give prepositions. Coherence alone therefore proves nothing.
- **Reading.** Invariance is direction-generic in Σ-whitened coordinates at both orders. Any direction's first-order effect is about 47% context-invariant (≈ φ_J = 0.52). Any pair's interaction is about 11% context-invariant (≈ φ_H = 0.11). The mean H-lens eigenvectors are special in energy, which the Σ prior places in the top PCs. They are not special in invariance. There is no privileged low-dimensional "H-space" at 0.8B L18 beyond Σ geometry. There is a weak, real, generic mean tensor.

### 4d. Does the mean Hessian help predict steering in unseen contexts? Barely
`hlens/hs_hpred.py`, `results/mlens/hpred_08b.json`.
- **Setup.** Six random Σ-shaped directions, each scaled to the typical centred state norm √tr Σ, so a move of size s is s·‖h‖. Fifteen pairs, 96 windows, one position per window.
- **Split.** Means are fit on even windows and scored on odd windows, and vice versa. Both halves agree to ±0.001.
- **Metrics.** R²_J uses the averaged single effects. R²_JH adds the averaged interaction r̄_ij.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| L12, 0.1 | 0.005 | 0.03 | 0.211 | +0.0004 |
| L12, 0.25 | 0.032 | 0.03 | 0.210 | +0.0025 |
| L12, 0.5 | 0.112 | 0.05 | 0.215 | **+0.011** |

- The averaged interaction recovers 5-17% of that. Net: +1.1-1.4 R² points at half-norm moves. That is just over the preset kill bar (0.01).
- **Reading.** At 0.8B the H-lens is a real but marginal predictive object.

### 4f. The 27B null is a heavy-tail artifact at L40/L52
`hlens/hs_mlens_trim.py`, `results/mlens/mlens_trim.json`.
- **Concentration.** At 27B the top 1% of rows by |y1||y2| carry 97-121% of the cross-moment energy. Above 100% means the other 99% net slightly negative.
- **Monster rows.** Their median scale is 54,000× the median row at L16, 382× at L40 and 135× at L52.
- **Tokens.** They are enriched 5-11× for digits ("1", "2", "9") and whitespace tokens (" ", " \n"). Positions are spread out (median 45-67 of 128), so they are not first-token massive activations.

## 4k. Is the mean Hessian a context-free law for how the J-lens moves? No (function words, 0.8B L18, $0)
`hlens/hs_fullh_jblind.py`, `results/fullh/fullh_jblind_08b_L18.json`, `out/jblind_08b.log`.

**Bar (preset).** Hbar_even applied to each held-out context's whitened state must explain ≥ 10% of that context's deviation from the mean gradient, and beat the 95th percentile of 200 sign-flipped means, for ≥ half the tokens.

**Result: 1 of 8 pass.** Only " ," passes, at 13.2% vs a null q95 of 10.4% (null max 22%). The rest explain −51% to +6%. Negative means the extrapolation Hbar · z overshoots: the Hessian is local, and states sit far from the mean.

**Descriptive** (near-tautological, flagged before running):
- The J-deflated dials are directions whose per-context first-order effect is 6-10× that of random deflated directions.
- That effect flips sign across contexts (|mean| / rms 0.05-0.36). So the J-lens averages it away.
- This follows mechanically from curvature, so it is not independent evidence.

**Post hoc, along the deflated dial coordinate only:**
- Hbar · z predicts 44-54% of the held-out variation for " and", " to" and " of", and 19% for " the".
- It fails badly for " .", " ,", " in" and " a".

**Reading.** The mean Hessian is not a global Taylor law for the J-lens over the state distribution. Its dials are real, local and causal, but they do not extrapolate as a rule.

## 4m. AND battery: the model computes ANDs, but almost none are context-free (preregistered, 0.8B, $0)
`PREREG-and-battery-2026-10-28.md` (amendments 1-6), `hlens/hs_and_items.py`, `hlens/hs_and_items_t2.py`, `hlens/hs_and_battery.py`, `results/and_battery/and_battery_08b.json`. Layers 6, 12, 18.

**Design.** 23 hand-built classes (AND and XOR logic), each in 2 independent phrasings: 46 class entries. Per item, a 2×2 finite interaction on the answer-contrast logit.
- **within**: both factors at the last position. Null = the larger SD of Σ-random pairs and realistic mismatched pairs from other classes.
- **cross**: factors at their own positions. Σ-random null only, the weaker null.
- **lensB / lensAB**: the item's factor A with the class-mean factor B̄ (leave one out), or both class means. This is the heart question: a context-free operator.
- **xferB / xferAB**: the same with B̄ taken from the other phrasing.
- **Tiers.** Tier 1 = a lens variant passes. Tier 2 = Tier 1 plus the small-ε contrast passes with scaling S_0.5 / S_0.25 in [2.5, 6] (4 = pure second order). Tier 3 = Tier 2 plus the answer token in the top-10 vocabulary decode.

## 5. Why the second-order mean can vanish when the first-order mean does not (theory sketch)
- **First order.** J_x = I + Σ_blocks (transport terms). The residual identity path gives every context the same large term. Block Jacobians are dominated by silu' ≥ −0.1 (mostly positive gates), attention-weighted value maps and norm projectors. All of these have nonzero means. So E_x J_x is large and the J-lens exists.
- **Second order.** There is no identity term. H_x = Σ_blocks [J_after · D²block · (J_before ⊗ J_before)] + transport cross-terms. Each D²block piece has a context-dependent sign or support:
  - SwiGLU: silu'' changes sign at z = 0 and z = ±2.4; the product term silu'·(w_g ⊗ w_u) is weighted by the downstream adjoint ρ_i(x).
  - Softmax: curvature is p(1−p)-type, centred on the attention pattern.
  - DeltaNet gates.
  - RMSNorm: terms along the current state n(x).
- Averaging over contexts mixes atoms with signs that vary by context. In a 27B model with about 10⁶ MLP atoms per layer plus mixers, the mean is a sum of many weakly correlated signed terms, while the per-context energy adds up the large active ones.
- **Prediction:** φ_H ≪ φ_J, with φ_H rising only where something pins the sign:
  - a fixed token (one gate pattern): observed, about 3×;
  - the final norm near the output: observed, rising with depth.
- This is an argument, not a proof. Result 3 is the direct check.

## 6. What this does to the project's claim ladder
**Established.**
- Second-order structure is real and causal within a context: v5 sign-flip, the v10/v11 punctuation H-space, and the §4i dials.
- Small models have a nonzero mean Hessian: φ_H 0.01-0.11 across four models and three depths, every CI excluding 0 (§4e).
- At 27B the context-invariant share is about zero at L16/L28. At L40 it is 0.012-0.015 after trimming the top 1% of rows, about 7× weaker than 0.8B (§4f). It is small, not empty.
- The model computes AND/XOR-type interactions (§4m), mostly in a context-specific way.

**Not established: H-space in the J-space sense.**
- The mean-Hessian dials are local. They are not a Taylor law for how the J-lens moves (§4k).
- No concept axis (§4j). No AND dictionary (§4l).
- The battery reaches Tier 1 only. Its one context-free operator (translation) is gate-like and not readable (§4m).
- The mean Hessian's top subspace misses the concept directions in both classes tested (§4n).

**Defensible claim today.** No J-like H-space was found in Qwen3.5-0.8B or Qwen3.6-27B at the tested resolution. Second-order computation is real but context-switched: which directions interact, and with what sign, changes with context.

**Not defensible.** "H-space cannot exist." §7 lists what is untested.

## 7. Moonshot and tight experiments
**Untested escape: invariance inside J-space ("door 1").**
- Every test above asks whether *total* curvature is shared. Most of that energy is in directions that are not readable content.
- The J-shaped question is narrower. Is the J × J block P_J H P_J (the ~25 readable directions per layer, on both sides) the same across contexts? Controls: same-size random and top-variance subspaces.
- A pass would mean a shared rule for combining verbalizable contents, even if total curvature is switched.
- Prior evidence leans against it: §4n's top mean-curvature subspace does not contain the concept directions. But §4n asks a different question, so it does not settle this one.

**What existing data can do.**
- The run-2 dumps (`hspace2_full_dump_L{16,28,40,52}.pt`) store HVP outputs y but not the tangent direction z or the readout c. So the exact J × J block cannot be computed from them.
- They do support a one-sided version at $0: φ_Jrow = tr(P_J M^lens P_J) / tr(P_J M^diag P_J), with the 27B `J25w` bases from `hspace2_full.pt` and the §2 estimator.
- Because ‖P_J T̄ P_J‖² ≤ ‖P_J T̄‖², a φ_Jrow near zero bounds door 1's numerator. It is not a full kill, because the denominator also shrinks.
- A φ_Jrow clearly above zero would justify one exact 27B run that saves z.
- Constraints for that run: the fla kernels likely do not support double backward, so it needs the torch fallback or gradient differences. One unit must be timed before billing.
